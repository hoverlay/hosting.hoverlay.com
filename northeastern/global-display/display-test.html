Additional hardware checks
  1. Repeat colors against dark, bright, patterned, and moving physical backgrounds; test daytime and evening lighting.
  2. Read names, dates and full factoids from touch distance and from the intended approach distance, straight on and from each side.
  3. Compare line widths, diagonal edges, moving dots, text sizes and glow. Choose the thinnest line that stays continuously visible.
  4. Check portrait orientation, native resolution, browser scaling, fullscreen margins and touch alignment at all four edges.
  5. Try one-finger drag, two-finger pinch, quick repeated taps, interruptions, accidental touches and idle return.
  6. Time reading and transitions with new viewers. Check animation smoothness, dropped frames, glare and distracting blinking.
  7. Run the complete sequence for an extended session; ask the manufacturer about static-content limits, dimming and panel-care cycles.
  8. Record panel model, brightness mode, lighting, background, distance, angle, chosen color and line width. Retest the final combination.

One-session recording guide

Keep the same panel brightness, background, viewing position and camera exposure while comparing versions. Use the native 1080 × 1920 portrait mode and 100% browser zoom. Record the test number and selected color in each clip. Test 4 preserves the original gradients, glow and fine-line styling; compare its atmosphere and readability with the three solid display versions.

  1. Record setup: panel model/label if available, brightness setting, room lights, what is behind the glass, browser scaling, viewing distance and camera angle.
  2. Test 1, 2, 3, then 4: record 10 seconds of the rotating globe in red, then 5 seconds in each remaining color: Blue, Purple, Pink, Orange, Cyan, Lime Green, White. Keep the camera still.
  3. On each version: choose Boston, let the factoids write in, then choose London while the cards are visible. Record their exit, camera movement, next factoids, and automatic return. Use the same color for this sequence on all four versions.
  4. Display Test tab: record every color on the sheet, close enough to judge 1 px lines and small text. Film the glow/solid pair and gradient without changing camera exposure.
  5. Change real conditions: repeat the best and worst colors with bright and dark physical backgrounds. Record front-on and roughly 30° from either side; show normal touch distance and the intended approach distance.
  6. Touch and readability: tap all five targets, drag/pinch the globe, and switch campuses rapidly. Say aloud anything that disappears, flickers, looks doubled, is hard to read, or misses a tap. Human observations matter more than camera color.

If possible, supply the original video files, not messaging-app compressed copies. Lock focus, white balance and exposure; avoid clipping white text. Do not judge colors from video alone. Run static test patches only long enough to record them, following the panel’s operating guidance.
